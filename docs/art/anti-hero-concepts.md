# The Anti-hero: three silhouettes

Owner: Art Director. Version 0, 2026-09-29. Concept art for Orb to choose from, not a locked design. He/him (Narrative). All names are working labels. Results below are **pending Legal review**.

**Sheets** (`art/concepts/anti-hero/`): `overview.svg` (all three side by side), `a-column.svg`, `b-bell.svg`, `c-standard.svg` (one sheet each: postures, wear, forms, palette, colour-vision and backdrop tests), `silhouette-test.svg` (flat black at true size; view at 100% zoom). They are generated by `gen.mjs`, so a change to a palette or a pose regenerates every sheet.

**Sources.** `docs/narrative/voices/anti-hero.md` (cold, formal, ranks people, the facade cracks), `docs/design/spec-wounds.md` (regions, stages, the Proud front, Humbled, Drop the Act), `docs/legal/q3-screen.md` and `fighter-concepts-review.md` (conditions), `docs/art/style-guide.md` (rules).

## 3. Posture states

Every sheet draws the same three postures in the same skeleton, so the concept is judged by its silhouette and clothes, not its pose.

| State | What the body does | What the face does | What it must read as at 40 px |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Proud** (the front) | Upright, weight back, chin raised, hands clasped behind the back, feet together | Half-lidded, flat brow, a flat mouth | A vertical, still figure |
| **Humbled** (struck, swallowing it) | Folded forward, chin down, one hand clutching the ribs, knees soft | Brow raised at the inner ends, eyes lowered, teeth gritted, a swallow mark at the throat, one bead of sweat | A hunched, bent figure |
| **Act dropped** (Drop the Act, unrestrained) | Wide stance, weight forward, chin out, arms open, coat or regalia flung back | Brow driven down, eye wide, mouth open, teeth showing | A wide, lunging figure |

Hair loosens in Humbled and Drop the Act (a lock falls over the brow) and never changes colour or shape into anything spiky. Contractions come back in his voice at the crack; the face and posture are the visual match for that (`voices/anti-hero.md`).

## 4. Wear progression

The wear stages and regions are `spec-wounds.md`: fresh under 30, bruised 30 to 59, battered 60 to 89, broken 90 and up, in head, core, arms and legs, plus the aura crown. Each sheet draws all four stages on the proud pose (the front hides the posture, the model still shows the damage).

**Wear map for C (the recommended design)**

| Region | Bruised | Battered | Broken |
|---|---|---|---|
| Head | Bruise at the cheek | Split lip, a brow cut with a blood run, hair loosens | Blood masks half the face, one eye shut, head hangs |
| Core (the pole rides here) | Scuffs on the harness and jacket | Jacket hem torn, a stain at the ribs, plates shorten and fray at the ends | Jacket torn open over a wound, the pole snaps and its top hangs, plates above the break are lost |
| Arms | Scuffs on the wraps | Wraps loosen and trail, bruise and cuts | The arm hangs, the wraps are gone, the hand is open and cannot close |
| Legs | Scuffs at the knee | Trouser hem torn | A limp, a bare cut on the shin, no dash pose |
| Aura crown | Steady | An arc flickers | An arc gaps |

A and B follow the same layers on their own regalia: A tears the coat hem, cracks a bracer and splits the collar; B rips the tier hems and the ruff. The sheets show them.

**The Proud front** (`spec-wounds.md` section 3): while Pride is at half or above, the animation holds the proud posture and a composed face at any wear stage, the crown stays whole and battered cards are withheld. The decals and regalia damage above still show. At the crack, all the withheld cues land in one beat: posture collapses (limp, guard drop, head hangs), the face changes, hair falls loose, breath shows, and the crown drops to its true state.

## 5. Animation and rig notes (for Animation, through the EP)

- **The front is a held pose, not an idle loop.** It ignores hit reactions below a threshold and returns to the composed pose. The crack is one authored transition, played once.
- **C's pole** is a rigid child of the spine at chest height, leaning with the torso. The six plates are short spring chains that lag behind velocity: they hang down at rest and stream straight back at speed (`STATE.sway` in the sheets, 0 to 1). Godot's spring bone node (SpringBoneSimulator3D) is the likely fit. Animation confirms.
- **The pole is also a readout.** Its lean shows torso lean at 12 px and less, which helps read hits and launches at the widest zoom.
- **A** needs cloth chains for the coat and sash. **B** needs three skirt chains and hides the legs, which hurts kick and knee readability in choreography.

## 6. Silhouette test

Flat black, at true size, with a bystander of the same height. Results from `silhouette-test.svg` viewed at 100% zoom and inspected at pixel level. **All results are pending Legal review.**

Sizes are body height, feet to hair. C's pole adds about 30%. At the widest zoom (0.06) a fighter is 4.5 px, so 5 px is the extreme. Real fights are 22 to 52 px.

| Test | A Column | B Bell | C Standard |
|---|---|---|---|
| 160 and 40 px, against the bystander | Pass. Tall collar and coat tail | Pass. The triangle | Pass. The pole and plate stack |
| 20 px | Marginal. The coat tail is about a pixel | Pass. The triangle | Pass. The pole line |
| 12 px, find the fighter among eleven bystanders | Fail. A slightly wider blob, easy to miss | Pass. The triangle stands out | Pass. The pole line above the head |
| 5 px, find the fighter among eleven bystanders | Fail. Indistinguishable | Marginal. A broader mass | Marginal. A tick 1 to 1.5 px taller |
| Three postures at 40 px | Pass | Pass | Pass |
| F1, F3 and F6 at 40 px | Fail. F1 to F5 look alike, only the coat changes it | Partial. The hem steps at F3, F4 and F6; F1 and F2 look alike | Pass. The plate count reads: 1, 3 and 6 |

**The shape feature (not colour) that tells each apart:** A, the coat tail and tall collar. B, the triangle. C, the pole above the head and the plate stack.

**What the test says beyond the three designs.** At 5 px, no silhouette rule carries identity for a slim figure. Only mass helps (B's triangle) and a 1 px extension helps a little (C's pole). So the far zoom must lean on the halo, the aura, and the colour mass, and the style guide's far beacon exists for that reason. It also says the value rule matters: without outlines, a dark violet figure vanishes on the sea at 5 to 12 px.

**Originality checklist, item 4 (shrink to a silhouette, then to three flat colours, "what does this remind me of?").** Answered honestly, and pending Legal:
- **A** reminds me of a long-coated duellist, or an officer in a greatcoat. No specific character came up. One note for Orb and Legal: a standing collar, rank bars and a sash have an officer-uniform reading. It is not a franchise echo, but it leans on the "ranking people" theme.
- **B** reminds me of a chess piece, a bell, or court dress. No specific character came up.
- **C** reminds me of a standard-bearer and of ceremonial back-banners on historic infantry and warriors. No specific character came up. It is a generic historic idea. Ours is a rigid pole with six rectangular plates, not a copy of any specific item.
- **I cannot run a fan-recognition check.** I only asked the checklist's question. Legal should look at all three and at the sheets, and run a reverse-image search on whichever design Orb picks.

## 7. Comparison and recommendation

| | A. Column | B. Bell | C. Standard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost (art and rigging) | Medium. Cloth plus many small pieces | Medium to high. Big cloth, three tiers | Low to medium. Rigid pieces, spring chains |
| Readability at 12 px | Fails | Passes | Passes |
| Readability at 40 px, forms and wear | Poor. Small pieces | Partial | Good. Plates and pole break in view |
| Legs and arms for choreography | Legs partly hidden | Legs hidden | Fully visible |
| Godot fit | Cloth bones | Cloth bones | Rigid meshes and spring bones |
| Animation load | Medium | High | Low |
| Distinct from the rest of the roster | Rectangle. Fine | A triangle. Close to the Empress' sweeping mantle | A line. Fine |
| Risk | Weak at small zoom. Officer-uniform reading | Confusable with the Empress. Hides the legs | Tone (a mast of rank flags is comic). About 30% more height in framing |

**Recommendation: C, the Standard. Orb decides.**

Why:
1. It is the only one that reads at 12 px and shows the form count and the wear at 40 px. The regalia count is the form readout.
2. Legs and arms stay visible, which matters for choreographed fights.
3. The animation and mesh load is lowest. The parts are rigid and the motion is a spring chain.
4. It keeps the roster's silhouettes apart: a line against a circle (Protagonist), a triangle (Empress) and a square (Cyborg).
5. The comedy fits Orb's tone (funny and sincere) and his voice: a man who ranks people wears a mast of rank flags. It stays sincere when the plates break in the crack.

What could go wrong:
- **Framing.** The pole adds about 30% of height. Camera must frame for it.
- **Tone.** If Orb wants a straight-faced Anti-hero, A is the fallback. It fails at 12 px, so it needs a halo and a bolt-on: C's pole works on any body. B is not recommended.
- **The plates might read as a wing or a comb** at some angles. The signal-flag arrangement (square ends, alternating colours, even spacing) was chosen to avoid that. Look at the proud pose on the C sheet.

## 8. Notes for other directors (through the EP)

- **Narrative:** `matchups.md` has the Empress saying "Do take that cape off. Ours is longer." to the Anti-hero. None of the three concepts wears a cape. With C the line could be "Do take that banner down. Ours is longer." Please choose.
- **Animation:** the front as a held pose, the crack as one beat, the spring chain on the plates (section 5).
- **VFX:** the aura and barrage colours on each sheet are proposals: near-white core, violet or rose rim. No red, no gold, no orange. A regalia break has no glow. Its boost is shown by posture and the aura brightening along his own violet ramp.
- **Rendering:** each regalia piece is its own mesh with an intact and a broken variant. Wear is a per-region float (style guide section 5).
- **Camera:** C is about 30% taller than the other fighters' silhouettes because of the pole.
- **Legal:** please screen the design Orb picks with the originality checklist. Notes for you: the pale wraps on C's forearms (bare hands, dark boots, so not the gloves-and-boots pairing); the crimson plates (a paint colour, hue about 336 degrees, no glow); A's swept lock (round-tipped, not flame-shaped).

## 9. Questions for Orb, ranked

1. **Which silhouette?** C (the Standard) is recommended. A is the straight-faced fallback. B is not recommended.
2. **Shed Regalia: yes or no?** It decides whether plates (or regalia pieces) are a mechanic that breaks for a boost, or only visible wear.
3. **The face and the build.** The concepts use a placeholder: an adult, lean, mid-brown skin, dark hair. Do you have a face, an age or a build in mind?
4. **A cape or not?** Narrative's line assumes one. The concepts have none. Change the line, or add a cape?
5. **How graphic on screen?** A three-way dial (Full, Reduced, Off) is designed. What is the default, and do civilians visibly die (`glossary.md` asks the same)?
6. **Is six the number of forms?** C's plate count is the form count. A seventh form means a seventh plate.
7. **Do you accept the hue lanes** (Protagonist teal, Anti-hero violet, Empress chartreuse, Cyborg red, fire owns orange and yellow)? See the style guide, section 3.3.
8. **Is the tone right for a mast of rank flags?** If you want him grim rather than wry, that points to A.
