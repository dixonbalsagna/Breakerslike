# The agency pass: options from Orb's first two-player playtest

Owner: Game Design. Orb played two local matches with a second player on 2026-10-02 (`docs/ep/vision.md`, last section). This page answers seven items from that playtest. **Nothing here is a final rule.** Each section gives options and a recommendation, and Orb's answers to questionnaire 14 decide. Every number is a proposal.

**Where the pillars bend.**
- *Pillar 2 (stances, not combos)* bends toward the player. The director still choreographs, but the player now sets the recipe (§2).
- *Pillar 3 (never out of range)* holds, with one change of meaning: an attack always **arrives**, but arriving no longer means the defender was frozen while it came (§1).
- The other pillars are untouched.

## 1. The ranged attack lockout

**The problem, in Orb's words:** "pressing a single attack key at any distance essentially locks both fighters from performing actions as the attacker flies in and performs the attack." Today the exchange is fixed at the press. The attacker is flown in by the director, and the defender's answer was read before the approach began.

### Six solutions

| # | Solution | The attacker during the approach | The defender during the approach | Cost |
| ---: | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| A | **The call-out** (Orb's idea). A press at range plays a taunt. Holding the attack through it launches him at top speed | Exposed for the taunt. Then committed to a straight charge, with a dodge-cancel as his only out | Free the whole time. He sees it coming and picks his answer | Every lazy attack from range starts about a second later |
| B | **The exchange starts at contact.** A press at range starts a rush, and nothing is decided until the first blow's wind-up | Steers the rush, and can drop it for free before the wind-up | Free until the wind-up: move, guard, dodge, blast or leave | Almost none. It is a change of when the director reads the fight |
| C | **Range decides the tool.** Beyond reach, a plain press is a blast from where he stands, and only a press with the stick held toward rushes | Fires and stays free, or chooses to rush | Free. A blast is answered as a blast | Physical players get energy pokes they didn't ask for |
| D | **The closing duel.** The approach becomes a small exchange: the defender picks meet, brace, sidestep or shoot, and the director resolves it at the meeting point | Committed, but the approach is now a read | Chooses one of four answers inside about half a second | More rules to learn, and the lock is still there, only filled |
| E | **Outs during the approach.** Keep today's rush, but the attacker may cancel for free and the defender's state is read at contact | Flown in by the director, with a free cancel | Changes his answer up to the wind-up, but can't move away | The smallest change, and it fixes least: he still can't leave |
| F | **A lunge range.** Attacks close the gap only inside about 12 bh. Further out a press whiffs, and players fly in themselves | Flies in by hand | Free | It breaks pillar 3. Not recommended |

**Recommendation: A and B together.** B is the real fix, because it removes the freeze at every range and costs nothing. A is Orb's idea, and it gives the long approach a face and a tell. With both, a press at range is a boast and then a charge, and the defender is never locked before the first wind-up.

### Orb's call-out, fleshed out

| Question | Proposal |
| :--- | :--- |
| **When a press is "at range"** | Beyond about 6 bh. Closer than that, a press attacks at once, as today |
| **One press** | He plays his taunt and stays where he is. The taunt rule is unchanged (`control-rules.md` §4): it takes 1 s, it can be dodge-cancelled in the first 20 ticks, and a completed taunt feeds his meter, once per 15 s |
| **Holding the press through the taunt** | When the taunt ends he takes off at top speed, about 1.5 times a normal rush, and strikes on arrival with the weight he pressed |
| **What the taunt gives** | The meter, as always, because the taunt completed. That keeps "taunts just feed meters": the charge is what the hold adds |
| **How it is punished** | He is open for the whole second. A hit during it is a clean hit at ×1.2, and a blast knocks him out of it. A rival can also use the second to charge, or to leave |
| **What the hold commits to** | A straight line to where the rival is. He can't guard on the way. His only out is a dodge-cancel for 15 ki |
| **The defender's answers to the charge** | Guard (a light bounces off, a heavy breaks the guard); dodge (he overshoots and is open); attack to meet it (a trade or a clash); a heavy blast, which stops the charge if it lands; or fly away, which becomes a chase (§6) |
| **During the cooldown** | He plays a short ready pose of 20 ticks in place of the taunt, with no meter, so a ranged press never does nothing |
| **Pillar 3** | It holds. The charge always reaches a rival who doesn't answer. Distance is never why an attack fails; the answer is |

**The quick ways in stay.** A blast needs no call-out (§5), and a player who flies in with the trigger (§6) and presses up close attacks at once. So the call-out is the showy, readable way to start a fight from range, and not the only one.

## 2. The fight alchemist

**Orb's direction:** presses are ingredients in the ongoing string, not a queue. The game reads the last few presses of both fighters, compares each player's running mix with their latest presses, and builds a sequence the player would expect. It must be clear and standardised, and holding, mashing and tapping in time must each give different results.

### What a press carries

Each attack press is one ingredient with four properties.

| Property | Values | Set by |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Weight | Light or heavy | The button |
| Family | Physical or energy | Whether RB is held (§5) |
| Direction | Toward, level or away, and the stick's tilt | The stick |
| Rhythm | Held, mashed or timed (below) | How it was pressed |

**The window** is each fighter's last three presses, and a press expires after 90 ticks. Presses aren't stored up: they change what the string does next, and unused ones fade.

### The recipes: what the mix means

The number of heavies in the last three sets the kind of string, and the last press sets how it ends.

| The last three | What plays | Feels like |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Three lights | **A flurry:** a rapid run of small strikes. Low damage each, quick to raise the mood | Speed |
| Two lights and a heavy | **A blur combo:** the rapid blur Orb describes, with one heavy accent that cracks a guard | The standard combo |
| One light and two heavies | **A bruiser string:** fewer, harder blows that push the rival back | Pressure |
| Three heavies | **A power blow:** one big, slow strike with a long wind-up | Commitment |

| The last press | The string's end |
| :--- | :--- |
| A light | It stays a brawl: both fighters remain in reach |
| A heavy | A knock-away. It becomes a launch only when one was earned (§3) |

**Direction.** Toward presses forward. Level stands and trades. Away gives ground: dodges, a heavy to knock the rival off, and blasts from range, which is the kiting Orb describes.

### Hold, mash and timed taps

| Rhythm | How it is read | What it does |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Held** | The button is down for 12 ticks or more | Power. The blow is charged: more damage, a longer wind-up, and it can break a guard or earn a launch |
| **Mashed** | Three presses inside 20 ticks, off the beat | Speed. More strikes, less damage each, and never a launch. It goes stale fast, and a perfect block beats it |
| **Timed** | A press within 4 ticks of a blow landing on screen | Precision. Each timed press is a clean hit for 10% more, and it keeps the string fresh. Three in a row earn a launch |

### The running mix

The game keeps each player's mix over their last 20 presses and compares it with their latest three.
- **A change-up:** the latest three break his habit, such as a heavy after a run of lights. Its first blow has a wind-up 3 ticks shorter, so it is harder to block.
- **A repeat:** the latest three match his habit. Wind-ups grow by 2 ticks for each repeat, up to 6, so he is easier to read. This is the staleness rule already in `control-rules.md` §6.

### When both fighters attack

| | He plays a flurry or a blur | He plays a bruiser string or a power blow |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I play a flurry or a blur** | A blur exchange: traded strikes, decided on the pulse | My lights land first. Three clean ones before his blow lands stop it; otherwise it comes through |
| **I play a bruiser string or a power blow** | The mirror of that | A fist clash, decided on the pulse |

If he isn't attacking, his held state decides, as today: guard, dodge, flying away, or nothing.

### What a player can always predict

1. The weight of my next blow is the weight I pressed.
2. More presses give more strikes.
3. Ending on a heavy sends him away. Ending on a light keeps him close.
4. Holding is bigger and slower. Mashing is faster and weaker. Timing is cleaner.
5. The stick decides which way he flies.
6. What I don't choose is the limb, the exact strike and the camera. That is the choreographer's.

### The stick and the launch

- When a blow launches or knocks away, the stick's tilt at that moment sets the direction **on the screen**: up, down, left, right or a diagonal.
- With no tilt, the planner picks the most dramatic direction, as today.
- The planner still picks the target inside about 45 degrees of the tilt (a building, water, a crater), within the collateral budgets. Depth stays the choreographer's (ADR 0009).
- In a juggle, each return blow reads the tilt again, so the player steers the pinball.

**What this replaces.** The queue of up to 3 presses in `control-rules.md` §6, and the director drawing chains by chance for human players.

## 3. Brawls against launches

**Orb:** "every brawl seems to end in a launch". Combo trading should be more represented and flashy, and juggles and pinballs slightly rarer, with higher payoff and spectacle. QA measures that 64.6% of exchanges end in a launch today, and about 20% of launches are caught in the air.

### Target shares

| How an exchange ends | Today | Target |
| :--- | ---: | :--- |
| **A brawl continues:** both fighters stay in reach | About 35%, with knock-backs | 40 to 50% |
| **A knock-back:** a shove of under about 8 bh, often a short skid along the ground | Not measured apart | 20 to 30% |
| **A launch:** a journey across the map | 64.6% | **25 to 35%** |

| Among launches | Today | Target |
| :--- | ---: | :--- |
| Juggles and pinballs (caught in the air, the ping-pong) | About 20% | 15 to 25%. With fewer launches that is about one a minute, down from about three |

### What earns a launch

A launch happens only when one of these is true. Otherwise a heavy gives a knock-back and a light keeps the brawl going.
1. A heavy ender after three or more strikes of the string landed.
2. Three timed presses in a row (§2).
3. A held, charged heavy that lands clean.
4. A guard break.
5. A riposte after a perfect block against a heavy or an ender.
6. A clash won on the pulse.
7. A throw.
8. A signature, a crippling blow or a finisher.
9. Any heavy on a rival who is staggered or opened up.

### Making the trade

- **Brawls get the flash:** more traded strings, the blur exchange, fist clashes and ripostes that keep the fight in reach.
- **Knock-backs bring back the short skid.** Orb saw one knockback slide in two fights. A knock-back on the ground is a short slide by default.
- **Launches pay more.** With about half as many, each one is worth more: its impact wear rises so that launches keep their share of the damage, and QA sizes the factor (about ×1.6) and re-tunes match length.
- **Juggles pay most.** The pinball keeps its rules (`control-rules.md` §11), and its ender always gets the panel.

## 4. Air recovery from knockbacks

**Orb:** "Players need a way to air recover from knockbacks." Today a fighter can only recover on the ground, on a bounce or in a tumble.

| | **A. The air brake** (recommended) | **B. The timed catch** | **C. Steering only** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Input** | A dodge tap during the flight | A dodge tap inside a short window near the top of the arc | The stick |
| **Window** | Any time from 12 ticks after the launch until the first contact. After an earned launch (§3) it opens at 20 ticks | A 10-tick window only | The whole flight |
| **What happens** | He rights himself over 10 ticks, keeps 40% of his speed and has control again | He rights himself at once, with a moment of safety | He bends his path by up to 15 degrees: toward water, away from a town |
| **Cost** | 15 ki, on the dodge-cancel's 3 s cooldown | Free, but a miss means no recovery until the ground | Free |
| **Counterplay** | The attacker's follow-up arrives before the window opens. A hit during the 10 ticks of righting is a clean hit. He recovers at a spot the attacker can see | The attacker varies the launch to move the window | None is needed |
| **Risk** | Too cheap, and launches stop mattering | Beginners never get it | It isn't a recovery |

**Recommendation: A, with C's steering added.** One button, always available, paid for, and answerable. The steering also lets a protector bend his own fall away from a town, which is in character.

**It never works** on a crippling blow's launch, a finisher's launch, or a hard impact (§7). The AI recovers on 20%, 50% or 80% of chances by difficulty.

## 5. Energy play

**Orb:** the beam cooldown "felt too restrictive, feels like a relic from the prototype". Bring energy into fights, with more ways to beam struggle and more forgiving timing to dodge and block beams. Perhaps hold RB to fire energy attacks.

### Hold RB for energy

- **While RB is held, attack presses are energy:** the light button fires a bolt or volley, and the heavy button a charged shot. Released, they are physical. This replaces the toggle.
- There is no hidden mode to forget, and mixing a blast into a string is one finger.
- RB can't be held together with the guard on LB, so firing and guarding are a choice.
- The Simple layout and touch keep choosing by range.

### Replacing the 120 s cooldown

| | **A. A beam gauge** (recommended) | **B. A short cooldown** | **C. Ki only** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The limit** | A gauge that fills from fighting: blows landed and taken, clashes and perfect blocks. A signature needs it full and still costs 45 ki | 25 s between signatures | Only the 45 ki and the 48-tick charge |
| **Pace** | About one signature per 45 to 60 s of real fighting, and faster for the fighter who is behind | One every 25 s at most | As fast as he can charge: one every few seconds |
| **Feels like** | Earned by fighting | A shorter relic | Spam, unless the cost rises |
| **Signatures a match** | 4 to 7 | 5 to 10 | 10 or more |

**Recommendation: A.** It replaces a clock with play. The last stand simply fills the gauge.

### More ways into a beam struggle

Today a struggle needs a signature answered by a signature.

1. **Streams.** Holding the heavy button with RB held fires a sustained stream, a lesser beam that drains about 15 ki a second. Stream against stream is a struggle, and so is a stream against a signature, which the stream starts at −10.
2. **A blast clash.** Two heavy shots that meet in flight make a short clash with one pulse.
3. **A late answer.** The defender may answer during the beam's wind-up, as today, or in the first 20 ticks after it fires. A late answer starts the struggle at −10.

**Target:** 3 to 6 struggles a match, up from 1 or 2.

### Forgiving timing against beams

| Answer | Today | Proposal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dodge** | A roll: the dodge works about 55% of the time | **No roll.** A dodge tap in the last 20 ticks of the wind-up or the first 10 ticks of the beam avoids it |
| **Perfect block** | The last 10 ticks of the wind-up | The last **14** ticks, for beams only |
| **Held guard** | Takes reduced damage | Unchanged |

### Walk through and split, brought forward

The three answers to a beam (swat, split, walk through) move to the first energy slice, ahead of the rest of the rule-of-cool list (`rule-of-cool.md`, feature 16). They need the perfect block against a beam, which exists, and VFX for the split. The placeholders can use them.

## 6. The left trigger and flight

**Orb:** escape tied to a direction and a held trigger "was a little strange". Players should fly at high speed at will, with a way to stop the other escaping and a way for the escaper to exit any situation at some risk.

### Flying fast at will

- **Hold LT to boost in any direction,** at about 2.2 times normal flight. A tap is still the dodge.
- Boosting isn't a stance any more. "Escape" is simply what happens when an attack arrives while he is boosting away.
- He can't guard, charge or fire while boosting.
- **A boost gauge** limits it: about 3 s of boost when full, refilling in 2 s when he stops. It is visible on his aura. Nobody can flee forever, and nobody is punished for a short dash.

### Stopping an escape

| Tool | How | Result |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The tackle** | The context button while boosting, within reach | A running grab that carries him to the ground |
| **A snaring shot** | A heavy blast that hits a boosting fighter | It knocks him out of the boost into a short tumble |
| **The chase** | Boost after him. His gauge runs down as fast as the chaser's | Whoever managed the gauge better arrives with boost to spare |
| **The planet** | Fly the other way round | He meets the rival coming, on a long escape. Pillar 1 doing the work |

**Recommendation: no dice in a chase.** Today a pursuit is a roll of about 50%. Replace it with these tools, so that a caught runner was caught by the other player. A fighter hit from behind while boosting takes a clean hit at ×1.2, and that hit counts as an earned launch.

### Exiting any situation, at a risk

| Situation | The exit | The risk |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Between an opponent's strikes | A dodge-cancel (15 ki), then hold LT to boost away | The ki, and the tackle or the snaring shot on the way out |
| While being hit | A burst (30 ki), then boost | The ki and the 8 s cooldown. A guarding rival absorbs the burst |
| In the air after a launch | The air brake (§4), then boost | The ki, and the 10 ticks of righting |
| In a crater (§7) | A burst, or wait it out | The ki, or the attacker's free second |

So every situation has an exit, every exit costs ki, and the boost that follows can be chased.

## 7. Hard impacts: left in the crater

**Orb:** an impact hard enough for an extremely large crater should leave the fighter in it, with no bounce out. Craters and bounce-and-crater impacts "look great", and terrain destruction is to be a central feature.

| Question | Proposal |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The threshold** | By the crater. An impact that digs a crater at least 1.5 bh deep embeds him. In today's terms that is a slam at about tier 3 or above, or any CRATER SLAM. Smaller impacts keep the bounce, the skid and the tumble |
| **What happens** | No hop, no bounce and no skid. He stops at the bottom, and the crater is the mark of it |
| **How long** | He is down for 60 ticks. He can guard from tick 20 |
| **How he gets out** | He rises by himself at 60 ticks. A burst (30 ki) throws him out from tick 20. He comes out with 10 ticks of safety, so he can't be embedded again at once |
| **What the attacker gets** | A free second, to spend one way: charge; reposition; taunt for meter; or **the follow-up dive**, a heavy into the crater that deepens it. The dive can be guarded or perfect-blocked from tick 20, and only one is allowed per embedding |
| **Limits** | The same fighter can't be embedded twice inside 5 s. The embedding still costs one impact of wear, and the dive is its own blow |
| **No air recovery** | A hard impact can't be braked (§4). That is part of what makes it hard |

**Why by crater and not by speed:** the player can see a crater. "A big enough hole holds you" is a rule that explains itself.

This replaces the single small hop after a very hard slam (`balance-targets.md` §20).
