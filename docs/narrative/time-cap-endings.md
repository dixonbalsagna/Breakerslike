# Time-cap endings: a story event forces the finish

Owner: Narrative and Fighter Identity. Version 1, 2026-09-30. Orb's brief: a match that reaches the 12-minute cap with no finisher ends with a **story event**: the planet or a third party intervenes to force an ending. No draw, no judges. Game Design sets the rule for who wins. All names are placeholders. Everything here is original, and none of it echoes a franchise.

## Three options

### A. The planet shrugs
**In one line:** the world has had enough. The cracks from the fighters' craters link up, and the crust gives way.
**On screen:** in the last minute the craters glow at the edges and thin lines of light run between them until they join into one web across the horizon. Tremors shake the camera. The ground tilts under the fighters, and the sea draws back. Then the plate they are on drops away in a long slide, and the last sequence is a chase along a collapsing floor. The planet's own sound (a deep groan, then a crack) replaces the music. The voice lines shrink to grunts.

### B. The Watch arrives
**In one line:** the people the fighters have been breaking finally send someone to stop them.
**On screen:** sirens rise over the wreckage, and long searchlights sweep the sky. A line of the planet's watch craft (placeholder: "the Watch") comes over the horizon, lowering cables and nets. They lock onto the fight and haul one of the fighters out of the sky. Both fighters stop mid-blow and look up. The mood is not comic: the crowd is finally on the scene, and the world has answered. The fighter the Watch reaches is the one Game Design's rule names.

### C. The people come back
**In one line:** the evacuated crowd returns, in a body, and stands in the fight's path.
**On screen:** from every ruined district, thousands of small figures walk back into the open and gather in a wide, silent ring around the fighters, and then close it. The fighters cannot swing without hitting them. The music drops out. The camera pulls back to show the ring, then pushes in on each fighter's face. The fight ends on whoever chooses to lower a fist and whoever does not. It plays on the game's collateral pillar: the people are what is at stake.

## A spare
**The falling moonlet.** The damage knocks a small low-orbit moon out of its path, and it falls slowly toward the planet in the last minute. On screen, a bright point grows in the sky while the fight goes on beneath it. It reuses the moonlets idea (option A4 of the orb pitches) and would suit a fight that has already reached the highest tier.

## How they read on screen, and which suits what

| Option | Who it suits | The feeling |
|---|---|---|
| **A. The planet shrugs** | Any matchup, best with heavy destruction | Awe and dread: the world is bigger than either of them. |
| **B. The Watch arrives** | A fight with a lot of collateral | Consequence: someone answers for the damage. |
| **C. The people come back** | A fight with the Protagonist, where the people matter | Quiet and moral, the opposite of a spectacle. |

## Notes for Game Design

- **The rule for who wins is yours.** Each option suggests a natural rule. A: whoever is on the surviving plate, or whoever makes the last decisive exchange while it falls. B: the Watch takes the fighter who has done the most damage to the planet. C: the fighter with the fewest casualties, or the one who lowers a fist first.
- **No draw and no judges.** Each event ends in a winner, and the scoring rule is hidden inside a scene, never a card of points.
- **Voices.** The dialogue director stays quiet during the event (a hush, then a few grunts, then a set piece line), and it can key a last line to the ending.
- **Legal.** A plain, original story beat. The Watch's ships should not resemble any franchise's fleet, and the crowd ring is a crowd, not a summons.
