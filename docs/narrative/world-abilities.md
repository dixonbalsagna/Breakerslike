# World-changing abilities: who each fighter is

Owner: Narrative and Fighter Identity. Version 1, 2026-09-30. Orb wants more ideas before assigning each fighter's world-changing ability: once per match, permanent for that match, reshaping the land, sky, water or the planet, or beyond. Game Design is pitching the mechanics. **These are character pitches**: three ideas per fighter that say who he or she is, what they would do to a world and why. Each has one line on what and why, and one on how it looks. Working labels, all placeholders. Original only.

## The Protagonist (earnest, protective, loves a real fight)

He changes the world so that nobody gets hurt and the fight can be big. All three are protective, and each protects in a different way.

| Idea | What he does, and why | How it looks |
|---|---|---|
| **Proving Ground** | He asks everyone to leave, then presses a wide region flat into a clean arena, so the fight has room and nobody is under it. He wants a fair fight, and a big one. | The land is pressed down in a great ring, dust rolling out, with raised rims like a bowl's edge. |
| **Shelter Ridge** | He lifts a ridge of stone between the fight and a town, so the town is safe for the rest of the match. He would rather move a mountain than lose a house. | Earth swells in a slow wave and stands up as a wall, with the town sitting quiet behind it. |
| **Land Bridge** | He raises the seabed into a causeway between two shores, so people have a road out. He cannot stop the fight, but he can give them a way to leave. | The water parts and a long spine of pale rock climbs out of the sea, wet and steaming, and joins the shores. |

## The Anti-hero (proud, hierarchical, needs a witness)

He reshapes the world into a stage that puts him above everyone, because rank is the only thing he trusts.

| Idea | What he does, and why | How it looks |
|---|---|---|
| **The Dais** | He raises the land into a stepped platform and stands on the top step, above the fight. Height is rank, and he wants everyone to feel it. | The ground rises in wide concentric steps, edges sharp and polished, with him alone at the crown. |
| **The Gallery** | He grows rings of stone seats around the fight, an empty arena for an audience. He needs a witness, and if there is none he will build the seats. | Rows of carved stone push up out of the rubble in a horseshoe, entirely empty, which is the joke and the sadness. |
| **The Mirror** | He freezes the sea into one flat, clear sheet, a floor worthy of a duel. It is cold, controlled, and it shows him his own reflection. | White cold spreads across the water in a long, silent sweep, until it is glass, and the fighters fall into it upside down. |

## The Empress (image-focused, imperial, annexes what she sees)

She reshapes the world as if she were tidying a province, because a planet is an empire she has not filed yet. Any paperwork stays in her voice, never on screen.

| Idea | What she does, and why | How it looks |
|---|---|---|
| **The Border** | She turns her decree line into a real border: a ruler-straight trench across the land, one side raised, one sunk, so the planet is now two provinces. She loves a clean line. | A perfectly straight cut runs from horizon to horizon, its edges neat as a ruled page, and the two halves settle at different heights. |
| **Golden Hour** | She stops the sun at the hour she looks best, so it is always low and gold. Her image is her power, and the planet will be lit for it. | The sun slides down and stays, long shadows lie across everything, and a warm haze glows on her train. |
| **The Palace** | She raises a huge, symmetrical palace complex from the ground: courtyards, terraces and gates, with her regalia carved into it. A world should have a capital. | Tidy terraces of gold-toned stone unfold outward in a perfect grid, gates opening in order, and the whole thing lit from within. |

## The Cyborg (a polite machine with an appetite)

He reshapes the world into a working machine, because a planet is a kitchen and he is hungry.

| Idea | What he does, and why | How it looks |
|---|---|---|
| **Central Kitchen** | He converts a district into a factory kitchen, permanent for the match. Ovens, conveyors and chimneys, all for his supper. | Buildings unfold into machinery, steam vents open, belts start turning, and a smell of toast drifts across the wreckage. |
| **Harvest** | He strips a biome of its trees and rock and lays it out as bare, tidy rows, so the land is stock. Hunger has no interest in scenery. | Trees fold into machines that sort them into rows, and the forest becomes a grid of dark fields in a minute. |
| **The Delivery Network** | He sets a ring of portal frames around the planet that stay open for the match, feeding his blitzes and his sandwiches everywhere at once. His restaurant now covers the whole world. | Mechanical frames of wire and gear stand at intervals across the land and sky, each lit with a soft ring, connected by faint lines. |

## The Cyborg's identity: hidden weapons, and the final form

1. **The hidden weapon.** He is the only fighter whose body hides his weapons: nothing on him looks like a weapon until it deploys, because he is a polite machine in a service smile, and a weapon is just a thing he has not opened yet.
2. **The final form.** When his technology comes fully online, it generates the weapons: the kitchen opens and the order is produced (the "special of the day"), so the last form is not a bigger body but the moment the restaurant finally serves what it was built to serve.
