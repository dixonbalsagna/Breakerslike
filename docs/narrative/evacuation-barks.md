# Evacuation barks

Owner: Narrative and Fighter Identity. Version 1, 2026-09-29. Companion to `line-system.md` (section 12) and World's `docs/world/collateral-caps.md`. Names are placeholders; lines are original and unsearched.

**The events.** When a tier's casualty budget is spent, people flee instead of dying: an `evacuate` event with a reason.
- **`budget`** is a **moment**: the streets are emptying, right now. Barks fire once per district per minute at most.
- **`ceiling`** is a **state**: no one is left to lose. It fires once when the ceiling is first reached, and then only as an occasional reminder.

**The roles.** The Protagonist is relieved. The villains are frustrated, each in his or her own voice: the Anti-hero contemptuous, the Empress affronted, the Cyborg sees the restaurant closing. Each has 5 lines per reason (40 lines), plus a narrator line for the first evacuation of a match.

## The Protagonist (relieved)

**Budget: a moment**
1. "They're getting out! Go, go, go, that's it!"
2. "Look at them run. Good. I'll keep it away from them."
3. "That's the streets clearing. Thank you, thank you."
4. "Everybody out! I'll deal with this in a second."
5. "Good. Good. Nobody's under that anymore."

**Ceiling: a state**
1. "There's nobody left to hurt. ...Okay. Now I can really go."
2. "That's everyone out. I'll say sorry to the city later."
3. "Empty. All of it. Nobody's here."
4. "Nobody left to lose. That's a relief, honestly."
5. "It's just us and the rubble now. Come on."

## Notes

- **Register still applies.** Against a world-ender the Protagonist's relief is quiet, not cheering. Against the Anti-hero, whom he is sparring, he is warmer. The line system's `register` tag selects the alternate.
- **The Cyborg's food.** Evacuation denies the Cyborg civilians to consume (it is his Hunger source), so his frustration is a design pressure as well as a joke. Game Design should check that the ceiling does not starve him in a way that unbalances the matchup.
- **Never show a person struck and alive.** The barks match World's rule: people who would die are removed and appear as runners. Lines refer to people leaving, never to people surviving a blast.
