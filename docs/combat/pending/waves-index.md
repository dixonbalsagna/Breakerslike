# The Anti-hero's moveset: the six waves on one page

For Orb. Owner: Combat and Choreography. Date: 2026-10-01.

Six sets of specs are written, and Animation poses from them. **None of it is in the game yet.** Together they need 83 new poses, plus 6 more that wait on the tail. Legal has cleared all six, and its conditions are written into the rows of each sheet. Animation's estimate for the whole moveset is about 295 poses and about 3 hours of your review (`docs/animation/review-plan.md`).

## What each wave adds

| Wave | What it adds, in plain words | New poses | What it needs before it can play |
| :--- | :--- | ---: | :--- |
| **1. Strikes** (`wave1-strikes.md`) | 38 different blows: punches, elbows, kicks, knees, a headbutt, shoulder hits and tail strikes. He stops repeating the same few. With a broken arm or leg he switches to the limbs that still work | 23 (and 4 for the tail) | The director choosing a blow for each hit. The 11 close blows (elbows, knees, headbutt, shoulder) also need the fighters brought closer than today's one distance. The broken limb's side from the sim |
| **2. Entries** (`wave2-entries.md`) | 15 ways of getting to the rival, picked by the direction you hold: six rushes, five ways of standing your ground, four retreats that counter | 12 | The director reading the held direction. Three need curved flight (the arc dive, the slide, the spiral). One needs fight lanes |
| **3. The ping-pong** (`wave3-pingpong.md`) | He knocks the rival away, outflies them on a curve, and knocks them back, up to five times, ending on one big blow. It also holds the one definition of curved flight that the camera and the effects share | 1 | Curved flight. Wave 1's strikes |
| **4. Grabs and throws** (`wave4-grabs.md`) | A grab, seven throws picked by the direction you hold, a running tackle, a dive grab, and a reversal out of guard. It includes the turning throw, which hurls a rival at something behind him | 22 (and 2 for the tail) | A "held" state in the sim, so a grabbed fighter is carried and cannot act. The grab on the context button |
| **5. Clashes and beam answers** (`wave5-clashes.md`) | Three clashes decided by timed presses: two heavy blows meeting, a flurry that travels across the sky, and a mid-air grapple. And three ways to answer a beam with a perfectly timed block: swat it aside, let it split around him, or walk through it | 18 | The timed-press pulse in the sim. The perfect block, which is built and waiting in a scratch copy. The grapple also needs wave 4 |
| **6. Energy** (`wave6-energy.md`) | 31 energy attacks in seven shapes: darts, fans, shards, crescents, a ring, a lobbed slab and a charged lance. Ranged play stops being only beams | 7 (and 6 hand shapes) | Energy mode in the sim. The seven shapes from VFX |

## The four things that stand in the way

| Blocker | Who | What waits on it |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Curved flight** (a path on the rush) | Encounter | three of wave 2's entries; all of wave 3 |
| **Fight lanes** | Simulation, World | one of wave 2's entries (the sidestep in depth) |
| **A held state** | Simulation, Encounter | all of wave 4; wave 5's grapple |
| **The tail** | **you** | 4 strikes, 1 throw and 1 feint: 6 poses |

Under all of it sits one more piece of work: the director picking pieces from these lists instead of playing fixed strings. That is Encounter's, and it is what makes the no-repeat and broken-limb rules real.

## The order they could go live in

1. **Wave 1's long and mid-range blows** (23 of the 34 that can be posed). They fit today's fighting distance, so they can show up as soon as they are posed, with no change to the sim. Which blow plays is still the animator's pick at this point, so the no-repeat and broken-limb rules are not yet enforced.
2. **The rest of wave 1**, once the director picks the blows and brings the fighters in for the close ones.
3. **Wave 2's eleven straight entries and wave 6's energy attacks.** Both arrive with the same step of the control scheme: the held direction and the mode.
4. **Wave 5's beam answers, fist clash and travelling flurry**, with the perfect block and the pulse.
5. **Curved flight:** wave 2's other three entries, then **wave 3's ping-pong**.
6. **The held state:** **wave 4's grabs and throws**, then wave 5's grapple.
7. The sidestep in depth with fight lanes, and the tail pieces after your ruling.

## The one decision that is yours: the tail

Legal is fine with a tail of hair, cloth or metal. If it goes, the "own limb" slot needs something else that uses neither an arm nor a leg. Without it he still never repeats a blow inside one string. But in his worst case (a broken leg, on the ground) his three-blow patterns would repeat in about 15% of strings, over Game Design's limit of 10%, and with a broken arm he would have one throw per direction instead of two.
