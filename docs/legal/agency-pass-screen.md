## 1. "Blow for Blow"

**The name.** It is an everyday idiom, so it is fine as an in-house label. It is also the title of a small HTML5 fighting-game prototype on itch.io, a 2025 film and a boxing programme (a "Blow by Blow"). Keep it a label for the set piece. Never use it as a title, tag or logo. Narrative may pick its own player-facing name.

**The scene.** I cannot compare against a scene I have not been told. If Orb names the series and the scene, tell Legal privately (not in a committed file) and I will compare it against these rules. Until then these rules assume the generic shape: two fighters standing and trading heavy blows in turn. The design is ours if it follows all of them.

**Staging rules for Combat (and Camera, Animation, Audio):**
1. **It starts from the exchange, not from an invitation.** The trigger is both fighters releasing a power blow on the flash. No fighter lowers his guard and offers a hit, and no "you first" or "hit me" beat.
2. **Each fighter takes the blow in his own way.** His own brace in his own stance and plates, not both standing arms-down. The Anti-hero's On the Chin arms-open stance stays his own move and does not appear here.
3. **A different strike each turn,** drawn from the fighter's own moves, heavy body blows to the core, never the same punch repeated and no face-punch cycle.
4. **No fixed alternating camera.** Not a repeating pair of profile or over-the-shoulder cuts with a freeze at each impact. Use the director's camera: wide shots that keep both bodies and the world in frame, with a push-in every second or third blow at most.
5. **No call and response.** No spoken pair like "Is that all?" then "Now my turn". At most one short line per fighter in the whole set piece, from his own bank.
6. **The world answers by biome, not by a ring.** A crater or crack under the blow is fine, but no ring of rubble rising and no lightning (the stacking rule).
7. **The ending is the fighter's own.** The one who gives way shows his wear stage and his own reaction, not a staged cough and kneel.
8. **Original sound and music,** no sound-alike of a known scene.
9. **The stacking rule applies** (`rule-of-cool-screen.md`): no scream, no flame aura, at most two of the seven marks in any moment.

## 2. Far taunt, the two charges, the range-band icon
- **Far taunt as a challenge:** a taunt line with a face cut-in, answered by a rush that meets him in the middle, is a staple. Clear. Taunt lines and gestures follow the earlier rules (no beckoning).
- **The two charges** (held light, held heavy): a flight at the rival. They follow the dash rule: not both arms trailed straight back, not one fist punched ahead, and he is drawn the whole way with no vanish.
- **Range-band icon:** a simple three-band marker is generic. Keep it a plain icon: no scanner look, no numeric readout and no radar sweep.

## 3. The first energy slice
- **Volleys that trade, both fire at once (a struggle on the pulse), walk through, split, swat:** staples, and the walk, split and swat are already screened (`rule-of-cool-screen.md`, RL-041 to RL-052). Clear.
- **Charged blast** (a held heavy that becomes a short beam): use each fighter's own charge look. For the Anti-hero it is plates stacking along the forearm. For the others, never a sphere growing in a palm, never hands at a hip, and never a two-hand push from the chest.
- **Hold RB for energy:** no issue.
