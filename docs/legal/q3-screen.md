## a. Orbs: what makes them safe

**What is not ours to use.** The franchise device is a fixed set of seven matching spheres, each marked with stars, hidden across the world, found with a sensor, and gathered to summon a being that grants a wish. Spheres of energy, orbiting energy, moons and glowing fragments are common and free. The set, the markings, the search and the wish are the signature. Because the game is titled "Orb Combat EX", a lookalike would read as deliberate, so the constraints below are firm. This replaces the earlier line "artefacts must not be orbs". Orbs are fine. That device is not.

**A3 Held fragments is CONDITIONAL on all of these:**
1. **Count.** Never a fixed set, and never a set of seven. The number on the field varies with the planet seed and the damage. Unlocking the relocation depends on total mass or charge held, not on holding a set number, so any mix of sizes works. No "collect them all" goal.
2. **Look.** Irregular molten or crystalline fragments of different sizes and colours, with cracks and heat. Not uniform, smooth, translucent amber or orange spheres. No stars, dots, numbers or any set of identical markings. Art's test: at thumbnail size, could a fan mistake a row of these for the franchise's set? If yes, redo them.
3. **How they are gathered.** They come from the planet being damaged, drop where it happens, and are contested: a heavy hit scatters one and the opponent can grab it. They are not hidden around the map to be searched for. No radar, sensor or "find them" device or UI.
4. **What they unlock.** Only what Orb described: enough held fragments let the Protagonist fold the fight into the barren proving ground. No summoned being, no wish, no dragon or spirit, and no reviving anyone with them. Call them fragments. Never "balls", never "wishes".
5. **Carrying pose.** They orbit his body. No pose with arms raised and a giant orb above the head (grey zone, `originality-rules.md`).
6. **Marketing.** No key art, logo, trailer frame or icon showing a hero holding a glowing orb aloft, or a row of matching orbs. An orb in a logo is fine, as long as it is not a sphere with stars.

**Tripwires.** If any of these appears, Legal returns NO-GO: exactly seven of anything; star or numeral markings; a sensor to find them; "wish" or "summon" language; a required order of collection; identical spheres scattered around the map.

**Fallback.** If Art or Marketing cannot keep clear of the tripwires, drop the carrying and the unlock. Keep A1 (the planet sheds fragments) and A2 (orbiting charge). Both are GO.

**A1 Core fragments: GO.** A damaged planet sheds molten orbs that anyone can grab for a charge surge. It is the arena's own mechanic. Same look rule as A3.
**A2 Orbit charge: GO.** A fighter's stored energy shown as small orbs circling him. Common in games. The pose rule in condition 5 applies.
**A4 Moonlets: GO.** Small moons knocked onto the planet.

## b. Overcommit: GO

A big boost that costs limb damage, shown as a glowing limb with fracture lines. It has no fixed times-N and no coloured aura. Keep it that way: no "x2, x3, x10" callouts, no red aura, no shout of a multiplier name. Routine checklist applies.

## c. Pass-Through: CONDITIONAL

The mechanism (a two-sided portal) is not the franchise's. The end result, people becoming food, is the franchise villain's gag, so the way it happens matters.
1. **No beam, no ray, no wand, no zap.** Civilians walk or are pulled through the portal. They are never transformed in place by a flourish.
2. **Sandwiches, from a machine.** The output is sandwiches from a process (a kitchen), not sweets, candy or chocolate, and never a target turned into an object where it stands.
3. **Frames are mechanical and destructible.** Not a swirling gate or a specific portal design or sound from the franchise's films.
4. **Tone.** The humour comes from the bureaucratic kitchen. Fine for the "mature" tone, but store content ratings and mobile store rules for graphic content are unchecked. Confirm before submission.

## d. Rail chip: GO

A chip moving between armour hatches. Keep the chip in a slot or carrier, never inside a translucent sphere or orb (the sphere core is a signature). The hatches are mechanical. Routine checklist applies.

## e. The ladders

The structure (tiers, triggers, costs, drain states, interruptions) is staple. The signatures to avoid stay avoided: a hair-colour ladder, a coloured multiplier aura, a moon-triggered beast, and a named final-form catchphrase.

**Protagonist, 3 tracks, 7 stages: CONDITIONAL.**
- The final reveal ("Open Hand") must not change hair colour or hair shape, and must not go white, gold or spiky and upswept. Show it with markings, eyes, posture and the proving-ground setting.
- No "x" multiplier callouts on the Pressure track, and no stage named with a "Super", "Ultra" or "God" style prefix.
- Keep the track triggers as designed. They are original.

**Anti-hero, 6 stages: GO.** Regalia must be designed fresh: not shoulder-pad armour with white gloves and boots, and not a flame-shaped hair silhouette.

**Tyrant, 12 revisions: CONDITIONAL.**
- The joke revisions (1 to 8) are original and GO.
- The real revisions (9 to 12) must not echo the franchise emperor's forms. The final form must not be a sleek, pale, slender humanoid with a purple accent and horns. Art picks a different palette and silhouette. Rev 9 and 10 armour must not recall a specific form of his.

**Cyborg, 3 molts: GO.** Do not follow the stage-by-stage look of the franchise's regenerating villain (larva, mid form, sleek "perfect" form with wings or a stinger tail). Wired, Kitchen and Docked look as designed: dark red, wiring, chain-mail, portal frames.

## f. Full Circle fusion: CONDITIONAL (look)

The trigger is original: flying opposite ways round a wrapped planet and merging when they meet, with no item and no pose. Fusion as an idea is common. The look needs care:
1. **No synchronised pose or dance,** and no mirrored matching poses before the merge.
2. **The merged fighter is its own design.** Not a mix of the two outfits and hair shapes of the pair (for example half of each hairstyle, or a vest that is the two costumes combined). A new silhouette.
3. **Rings, not a halo.** Counter-rotating rings around the body are fine. Avoid a halo shape above the head.
4. **No earrings, no accessory** in any part of the trigger or the look.
5. **The timed merge** (a pride crash afterwards) is fine as a drain state. Do not call it by a franchise-style fusion name.

## g. Stylus tail: GO

A segmented metal tail ending in a pen nib is not the franchise's tail in silhouette or function. Keep it segmented and metallic, with the nib, not a reptilian or furred tail.

## Working labels as names

Stage 0 only (franchise words and habits): none of the labels contains a franchise word or follows its naming habits. Notes: **Apex** and **Wired** are also brand words (a game brand and a magazine), fine as in-game labels and never in a title, tag or logo. Overcommit, Pass-Through, Rail chip, Full Circle, Held fragments, Orbit charge, Stylus tail, Answer, Full Measure, Weathered, Rooted, Breakpoint, Open Hand, Poise, Regalia, Hierarchy, Wrath, Sovereign, Interlace, Borrowed Strength, Kitchen and Docked are ordinary phrases. These are labels, so the label exception applies (`name-screening.md`). No Steam or web search was run. Run a Steam look before any of them goes into marketing.
