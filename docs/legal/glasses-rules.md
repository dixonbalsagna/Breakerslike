# The Anti-hero's glasses and glare: rules in advance

Owner: Legal and IP Compliance. 2026-10-02. For Art and VFX, before the frames and glare states are drawn (`art/concepts/refine/anti-hero-glasses.svg`). A screen, not legal advice.

**Verdict: GO.** Glasses that flash opaque with glare are a stock anime trope, free to use. Several well-known characters own particular versions of it, so the rules below keep ours generic and ours.

## What to avoid
Specific versions that stack the trope into one character's signature:
- A cold commander who hides his eyes behind glinting tinted lenses and folded hands before his face.
- A boy detective whose glasses flash white when he deduces the answer.
- A scheming doctor with small round lenses that glint on a sly smile.
- A calculating "data" character who pushes up his glasses and recites a probability.

## Frame shapes
1. **Our own shape language.** The Anti-hero is blades and hexagons. Use an angular, tapered or hexagonal lens, cut asymmetrically, with a thin frame in his violet or a steel tone.
2. **Not these:** thin round wire lenses, narrow orange or amber tinted rectangles, or a half-rim in the tinted-rectangle style. No goatee or beard with the glasses.
3. Lenses are never a display. No readout, overlay, scan bar or side panel on a lens (that is the scouter idea).

## The gesture
- The push-up of the glasses at the bridge is generic, but the stock version (one finger at the bridge, lens flashing, silent stare) is the most copied. **Make it his own:** a nudge at the temple arm, or the back of the forearm plate, never a middle finger.
- **Never with folded or steepled hands in front of the face or mouth.** Never with elbows propped and hands clasped.

## The glare
1. **Shape:** a hard-edged glint cut in his blade language, a flat wedge or a sweeping slash across the lens, never a round white disc with a star twinkle. The lens goes opaque for a beat, then clears.
2. **Colour:** a pale tint of his own violet, not pure white, and never gold, orange or red. Normal lenses are a dark violet tint, not orange.
3. **Timing:** short. A quick attack and a hold of 0.4 s at most. At key moments only: reading a move, pride, a taunt, the seal break (cracked lens). Never every exchange.
4. **Never at a transformation break,** and never with a shouted form name. That is how a white flash becomes mark 7 of the stacking rule.

## Lines said with it
Avoid the stock lines that go with the trope: "just as planned", "everything is going according to plan", "as I calculated", "it's exactly as I predicted", a sly chuckle as a signature, and any spoken probability or percentage. His lines come from Narrative's bank in his own voice and get the usual exact-phrase search.

## How the glare counts against the stacking rule
- The glare is **face-local and brief.** In our tint, with no shouted form name and away from a transformation, it counts as **no mark**.
- If it is white-ish and meets a shouted form name or a break flash, it counts as mark 7.
- It may sit beside **one** other mark, never two. In practice: no glare during a crouch-and-scream charge, a rubble ring or lightning.
- A cracked lens or glasses knocked off by a hit are fine and count as no mark.
