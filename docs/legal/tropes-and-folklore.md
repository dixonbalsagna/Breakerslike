# Tropes, folklore and how close is too close

Written for Orb, 2026-09-30. Plain language, not legal advice. Franchise names appear here as analysis, never as design references. Counsel should look at the closest items before a store page.

## 1. Idea against expression

**Free to use, because the genre shares it:** glowing power-up forms, auras, energy beams, beam struggles, flight, collecting artefacts to unlock something, rival heroes, transformations that cost something, worlds that break.

**Protected:** specific designs (how a character looks), names, lines, music, art, and a **combination of distinctive features** that together make a character recognisable. The law asks whether an ordinary person sees the same expression, not whether the same idea is present.

## 2. Why a golden super form in a platformer can sit beside the franchise's

A golden power-up form, with a gem-collecting unlock, exists in a famous platformer that appeared in the same era. As far as we know it has lived beside the franchise without a public fight. That is not proof it is safe, but the reasons are instructive: a glowing power-up is a trope, the overall expression is different (a blue hedgehog, not a spiky-haired martial artist), and the form belongs to that publisher's own character. **The line is stacking.** One borrowed feature is a trope. Gold spiky hair, plus a flame aura, plus a scream, plus a "Super" name, plus a set of seven gems, plus an orange uniform is one character's signature, and that is copying however each piece is justified.

## 3. Fair use is the wrong frame for us

Fair use is a defence you argue when you are accused of copying. An original commercial game should not need it, because it does not copy. If we ever lean on it, we have already lost the safe ground. See `tribute-vs-parody.md`. **"Homage" safely means:** we share ideas, conventions and feel, and we may say in plain words that the genre inspired us. It never means reusing names, designs, lines, music or a recognisable combination.

## 4. Trademark: what we never use

Names of techniques (the hip-fired wave, the giant orb of gathered energy, the spinning disc, and the rest), form names ("Super ___", the rage-form names, the "ultra" forms), character names, the franchise titles and their letters ("Z", "GT", "Kai"), "Saiyan", and any logo. Trademark protects names and logos even when no art is copied, and it does not expire.

## 5. Public-domain folklore: draw from the source

**Usable:** the 16th-century monkey-king novel *Journey to the West* and its cast, plus Greek, Norse, Arthurian, Ramayana and Mahabharata, Gilgamesh, Shahnameh and Japanese folk tales, Daoist texts on qi and inner alchemy, and classical meridian theory. Also the broad genre furniture of wuxia and cultivation stories (martial sects, lightness skill, hidden manuals, tribulation lightning).

**Rule: use the source, not a modern retelling's take.** Read the text (in the original or a public-domain translation, such as a 1913 one) and build from it. Modern translations, films, games and adaptations are separately protected, and their specific designs and inventions are off limits.

| Safe | Risky |
|---|---|
| A staff that changes size, a cloud that carries its rider a huge distance, seventy-two changes, a golden headband, peaches of immortality, rebellion in a heavenly court, a mountain pressed on a rebel (all in the novel) | The franchise's specific versions: a named nimbus cloud, a named extending pole, a tailed orange-clad fighter, the Japanese reading of the monkey king's name as a hero's name |
| Qi, meridians, inner alchemy, realms of practice as ideas | A specific modern web novel's named realm ladder and techniques |
| Martial sects and hidden manuals in general | Characters, techniques and lines from a named modern wuxia author (Jin Yong and Gu Long are **not** public domain) |
| A trickster hero built from the novel's text | A look that combines the monkey-king's features in the franchise's way |

**Two more cautions.** A public-domain name can still be someone else's trademark in games (several games use "Wukong" or "Monkey King" as a title), so a name still goes through `name-screening.md`. And some sources carry cultural weight, so treat them with care.

## 6. The orb question

`q3-screen.md` (section a and round 2) allows orbs with constraints: the planet sheds irregular molten or crystalline fragments of varied sizes, contested and never a fixed set and never seven; no star or numeral markings; no radar; no wish, summoning or revival; they orbit the hero and unlock only the fold into the proving ground; the payoff is an inward fold, never a column of light into a darkening sky. **There is plenty of room inside that**: glowing, carried, fought over, a big cinematic payoff, a race to reach a fragment, and the whole cast wanting them. The title "Orb Combat EX" is fine. The tripwires are the franchise's staging, not the round shape.
